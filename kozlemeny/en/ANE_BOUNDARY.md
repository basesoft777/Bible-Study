## What the work does not contain

The derivation of the motifs stays **within the biblical corpus**: the Hebrew Old Testament, the Greek New Testament and the Greek translation of the Septuagint. The work **contains no ancient Near Eastern comparative layer**: it queries no Ugaritic, Mesopotamian (Akkadian, Sumerian) or Egyptian text, and no such text collection is among the sources used.

## Why

The method examines how a motif lives in the biblical text: at which passages it occurs, on which shared Hebrew or Greek words, with what function, and how it passes from the Old Testament through the Septuagint into the New Testament. For this the biblical text and the lexica suffice, and these queries are fully reproducible. An ancient Near Eastern comparison requires other sources (cuneiform and Ugaritic text editions), other expertise and another standard of proof; the present working process does not carry it out.

## What this means for the reader

- **The scope of the claims is the biblical corpus.** What a page says about the occurrences, distribution or function of a motif refers to the biblical text. The page does not claim that the motif has no Near Eastern parallel or antecedent, nor that it has one.
- **Where the prose nevertheless mentions a Near Eastern background**, it does so as interpretation, following secondary literature or lexical etymology; it is not the result of the queries, and we have not checked it against the original Near Eastern texts. Such mentions currently appear, for example, on the Tehom page (the debate on the kinship with Tiāmat in the Akkadian creation epic, *Enūma Eliš*) and on the Rephaim page (the Ugaritic *rāpiʾūma* texts).
- **The most affected motifs:** Tehom (the primordial deep), Hades (Sheol) and the Rephaim (*rĕpāʾîm*). For these, international scholarship expects the inclusion of Near Eastern, chiefly Ugaritic, material; a reader of that kind should read these pages with the boundary stated above.
- **The absence is not a negative finding.** That a page offers no Near Eastern parallel is a limit of the method, not a result of the investigation.
